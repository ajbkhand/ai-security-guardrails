# Defra AI Security Guardrails

These guardrails define the **minimum security expectations** for AI systems across Defra.

They apply to:
- All AI workloads (models, pipelines, applications)
- All platforms (Azure, AWS, CDP, DASH, suppliers)
- All lifecycle stages (Concept → Scale)

## Why this matters

AI systems introduce new risks due to:

- Non-deterministic behaviour  
- Dependence on large datasets  
- Ability to interact with users and systems  ---

## What you need to do

Before building any AI system, you must:

- ✅ Confirm AI is necessary  
- ✅ Identify the risk level  
- ✅ Apply the correct guardrail tier  
- ✅ Follow the lifecycle  
- ✅ Test before release  
- ✅ Monitor after deployment

## Apply the right controls

All AI systems must:

- Apply **Tier 2 minimum controls**  
- Add additional controls for higher-risk systems

## Follow the lifecycle

You must apply controls at each stage:

- Concept → define risks  
- Incubate → design securely  
- Pilot → test and validate  
- Scale → monitor and govern

## Understand your responsibilities

Different roles must take different actions.

- Programme leads → own risk  
- Project managers → embed controls  
- Technologists → implement security

## Understand AI risks

AI systems introduce new threats:

- Prompt injection  
- Data poisoning  
- Model misuse  

👉 {{ '/attack-vectors/' | relative_url }}Understand AI risks

---

## Do

- ✅ Use AI only where necessary  
- ✅ Apply controls based on risk  
- ✅ Test systems before release  
- ✅ Monitor systems continuously  

## Do not

- ❌ Build AI without understanding risks  
- ❌ Skip testing or governance  
- ❌ Deploy without monitoring  

---

> AI must be secure, transparent and proportionate to risk.

This creates risks including:
- Prompt injection  
- Data leakage  
- Model misuse  

## Core principle

> AI is a technology category. Security must be consistent across all implementations.
